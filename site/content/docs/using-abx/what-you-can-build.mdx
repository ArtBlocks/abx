**ABX: the X is for anything.**

Generative art got here first. It won't be the last thing. An ABX project is a programmable object that
lives onchain. It can host a website, run a game, keep time, lock up money, remember who showed up, and
fire off transactions of its own.

Every ABX project is a standard ERC-721 or ERC-1155 contract, so wallets, marketplaces, and indexers
already know what to do with it. What it holds, what it does, and who gets to change it is up to you.

## Objects that act

Most NFTs just sit there. An ABX object can do things. Attach a hook to a code project, and every mint,
transfer, burn, or setting change can set off onchain actions in the same transaction:

- Launch a token through a protocol's factory.
- Swap into or out of a pool.
- Mint a piece from another collection.
- Pay a treasury, stake, vote, or settle a position.
- Say no, and block the action, when the rules aren't met.

If a side effect fails, the whole action rolls back. Nothing half-happens. A hook is a contract you
write, so whatever a contract can do onchain, a hook can do.
[Custom mechanics](/docs/using-abx/guides/custom-mechanics) shows how.

## What people are building

Plenty of generative art. Plenty that isn't:

- **Objects that hold money.** A card with its own wallet and its own loan that shakes as the loan
  nears liquidation. A vault that won't open until a date. Exchange positions that settle in turn.
- **Live things and games.** Clocks that show the real sky. A puzzle that changes every day with no
  server. Creatures that grow with every click. A game that keeps every run onchain and hands a crown
  to the new top score.
- **Sites and media.** A radio station with no server and no audio files. A block explorer stored
  onchain. Social profiles with their own wallets and token-gated chat.
- **People and promises.** Event guestbooks. Donor recognition that grows with each gift. A punch card
  for everyone you met. A vow that closes when you keep it. A score written by a community.
- **Agents.** Agents logging what they see. Agents making and minting their own work.
- **Art.** Generative series, editions, and 1/1s, onchain and off.

The best ABX idea hasn't been thought of yet.

## Prompts to steal

Paste one into your agent and change it until it's yours:

- "Help me put my band's website onchain and sell the album as an edition."
- "Help me make a clock for my hometown that shows the real sky."
- "Help me make a guestbook for my event where every guest gets a token."
- "Help me make a vault that locks ETH until a date, with a certificate that counts down."
- "Help me make a membership pass whose level only goes up and that can't be sold."
- "Help me make a ticket that swaps part of its mint price into our community token."
- "Help me make a pass that launches a token for its holder when they flip a switch."
- "Help me make a daily puzzle that changes at midnight UTC without a server."

Tell the agent what the thing is for, who uses it, what changes over time, and who gets to change it.
Looks come later. With the [ABX skill](/docs/using-abx/agent-and-skill), the agent explores ideas with
you and waits for you to pick one before it builds anything.

## Before you build

- These are patterns, not buttons. Rules and side effects live in a contract you write and test on a
  fork or testnet.
- Hooks attach to code projects created with `abx deploy-code`. An image series can't add them later.
- A hook spends its own funds and approvals. It never gets the holder's wallet.
- A token can stand for rights or money, but real-world rights, custody, and enforcement need their own
  legal design.
- Putting code onchain doesn't put the services it calls onchain. Plan for each one.
